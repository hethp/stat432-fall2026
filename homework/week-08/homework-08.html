Question 1: Boundary and margin

We use margin-geometry-20.csv with 20 observations having predictors x1, x2 and class labels y ∈ {−1, 1}. The linear score is

f(x) = 0.8 x1 + 0.6 x2.

Since ‖(0.8, 0.6)‖2 = 1, the distance from an observation to the decision boundary f(x) = 0 is simply |f(x)|, and the functional margin is y f(x). An observation is inside the margin when y f(x) < 1, on the margin when y f(x) = 1, and beyond the margin when y f(x) > 1.

Question 2: Margin in four dimensions

We use margin-geometry-4d.csv with 50 observations having four predictors and class labels y ∈ {−1, 1}. The linear score is

f(x) = β0 + xTβ,   β0 = −1,   β = (1, 2, −2, 4)T.

Part (a): Projection and geometric margin

The norm of β is ‖β‖ = √(12 + 22 + 22 + 42) = √25 = 5.

The unit direction is βˆ = β / ‖β‖ = (0.2, 0.4, −0.4, 0.8).

Each observation is projected onto this direction: proji = xiT βˆ. The decision boundary f(x) = 0 corresponds to proj = −β0 / ‖β‖ = 1/5 = 0.2 in projection space.

The observations are projected onto the unit direction βˆ = (0.2, 0.4, −0.4, 0.8). The decision boundary sits at projection value 0.2 (= −β0/‖β‖ = 1/5).

From the projection plot, the closest class 1 observations to the boundary are observations 4 and 33 with projections of 1.2, and the closest class −1 observations are observations 15 and 36 with projections of −0.8.

The distance from the boundary (at proj = 0.2) to the closest observations:

  • Class 1 closest: |1.2 − 0.2| = 1.0
  • Class −1 closest: |−0.8 − 0.2| = 1.0
Geometric margin: M = 1.0.
This can also be computed as M = mini yi f(xi) / ‖β‖ = 5 / 5 = 1.

Part (b): SVM normalization

The SVM normalization rescales (β, β0) so that mini yi f(xi) = 1.

Currently, the minimum functional margin is mini yi f(xi) = 5 (achieved by observations 4, 15, 33, and 36). We divide all parameters by 5:

SVM-normalized parameters:

β0* = −1/5 = −0.2
β* = (1/5, 2/5, −2/5, 4/5) = (0.2, 0.4, −0.4, 0.8)

Verification: Under this normalization, ‖β*‖ = √(0.04 + 0.16 + 0.16 + 0.64) = √1 = 1, and the geometric margin is M = 1 / ‖β*‖ = 1 / 1 = 1, consistent with part (a).
